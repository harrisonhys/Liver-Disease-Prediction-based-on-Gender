Stage 1 - Business Understanding

Project Title:
Analysis of Machine Learning Model Performance Differences in Liver Disease Prediction Based on Gender

Problem Statement:
Machine learning models for healthcare prediction are often evaluated using aggregate performance metrics. In practice, aggregate evaluation can hide subgroup-specific weaknesses. In this project, liver disease prediction performance may differ between male and female patients, and such differences may indicate fairness concerns or clinically important blind spots.

Business and Research Objective:
Develop a reproducible machine learning workflow that predicts liver disease and evaluates whether predictive performance differs across gender subgroups. The project aims to support a fairness-aware assessment of healthcare AI models and generate results suitable for academic reporting.

Primary Objectives:
1. Evaluate overall classification performance for liver disease prediction.
2. Measure performance separately for male and female patients.
3. Compare performance gaps between gender subgroups.
4. Examine whether feature importance patterns differ across gender.
5. Identify whether model behavior suggests potential fairness concerns.

Analytical Scope:
- Dataset: Indian Liver Patient Dataset (ILPD)
- Task: Binary classification
- Candidate models: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost
- Evaluation dimensions: predictive accuracy, subgroup performance, fairness gap, interpretability

Expected Value:
The study provides evidence on whether aggregate evaluation is sufficient for liver disease prediction and whether subgroup analysis adds clinically relevant insight for model assessment.
