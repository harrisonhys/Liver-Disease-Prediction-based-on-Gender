Stage 1 - Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1:
There is a measurable predictive performance difference between male and female subgroups in liver disease classification.

Rationale:
Differences in subgroup size, feature distribution, or class distribution may lead to unequal model performance.

Hypothesis 2:
Feature importance differs across gender subgroups.

Rationale:
Clinical indicators may contribute differently to liver disease prediction in male and female patients, which may be reflected in model interpretation outputs.

Hypothesis 3:
Subgroup evaluation provides more informative insight than aggregate evaluation alone.

Rationale:
Aggregate metrics can obscure disparities in recall, false negative rate, or overall classification quality for smaller or differently distributed groups.

Hypothesis 4:
At least one model will show a fairness-relevant gap in recall or false negative rate across gender.

Rationale:
In healthcare classification, missed positive cases are especially important. If one subgroup has systematically lower recall, the model may introduce practical clinical risk.

Success Criteria for Later Stages:
1. Produce overall and subgroup metrics for all candidate models.
2. Quantify fairness-related performance gaps.
3. Interpret whether the observed gaps are meaningful for healthcare decision support.
4. Generate publication-ready reports, tables, and plots.
